The joke collectives

122 entries on this site are marked as jokes. Nearly all come from one comic: a mock Victorian chart of group names for ghosts, demons, robots and other creatures that never travel in herds.

Wondermark #566

On 30 October 2009, the eve of Halloween, the cartoonist David Malki ! published Wondermark #566, “The Stoakes-Whibley Natural Index of Supernatural Collective Nouns”, subtitled “for holiday or every day”. Wondermark is built from 19th-century woodcuts and engravings, and this strip takes the form of a page from an old natural-history reference: a list of “a [something] of [creatures]”, sorted under headings such as Spectral, Demon, Elven, Heavenly, Mechanical, Monster and Undead.

The entries are puns on each creature: a congress of ghosts, a basement of vampires, a clangor of robots. That is the same game the medieval lists played. The 1486 Book of Saint Albans already had a superfluity of nuns and an abominable sight of monks, which still turn up in lists of collective nouns today.

The comic is still online at wondermark.com, where it is also sold as a poster. It is © David Malki !, and this site lists the terms with a link back to the strip rather than reproducing it.

How they are marked

Joke entries carry a purple Joke label on word and subject pages, and appear in purple italics on the category pages. The Jokes button on the word and subject indexes shows only these.

A few terms in the comic are real collective nouns found in dictionaries, so they are listed here as ordinary entries rather than jokes: a coven of witches · a host of angels · a pantheon of gods.
